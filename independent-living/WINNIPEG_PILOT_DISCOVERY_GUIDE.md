# Winnipeg Pilot Discovery Guide

**Purpose:** Validate a real problem and willingness to pay before building more software. This is research, not a sales pitch. Do not collect diagnoses, medication details, health-card numbers, exact home addresses, or other unnecessary sensitive information.

## Who to interview
- 15 older adults, people with disabilities, or family caregivers who have arranged practical non-clinical support in the last 6 months.
- 15 local providers across non-emergency transportation, companionship, errands/grocery support, and light household assistance.
- Include people who chose not to use a service, not only satisfied customers.

## Family / customer interview (20 minutes)
1. Tell me about the last time you needed help with a ride, errand, household task, or social visit.
2. What triggered the need, and how did you find someone?
3. What options did you consider? Which did you reject, and why?
4. How long did it take to arrange? What was frustrating or stressful?
5. What did the service cost? Who paid?
6. What made you trust—or distrust—the provider?
7. If you needed this again next week, what would you do?
8. Have you ever coordinated this for someone else? How did you get their permission and keep them involved?
9. What would make a service-matching option genuinely useful? What would make you avoid it?
10. Would you pay a clearly stated booking/coordination fee, or would you expect the provider to pay? What would feel fair? Ask for a concrete range only after understanding their current spending.

**Close:** May we contact you once with a short update about the pilot? Record consent and preferred contact method separately. No pre-checked marketing consent.

## Provider interview (20 minutes)
1. Which services do you actually provide, and in which Winnipeg neighbourhoods/areas?
2. Who is your ideal customer? What requests are outside your scope?
3. How do customers currently find and book you?
4. What is your current availability and capacity? How quickly can you respond to a referral?
5. How do you price the work (minimum, hourly, mileage, quote)?
6. What insurance, licences, training, screening, references, or vehicle requirements apply to your service? Can you provide current evidence?
7. What would make a referral useful rather than an administrative burden?
8. Would you accept a small number of manually matched pilot referrals? Under what conditions?
9. What fee model would be acceptable, and who should pay it?
10. What would cause you to decline or stop participating?

**Close:** Ask permission before retaining business contact details or sending follow-up. Do not describe a provider as verified until checks are complete and documented.

## Evidence log
For each interview record:
- Interview code (not full name)
- Participant type (customer / family supporter / provider)
- Service category and broad service area
- Problem described in the participant's own words (brief, non-sensitive)
- Current workaround and actual cost, if volunteered
- Frequency / urgency
- Trust and safety concerns
- Evidence of willingness to pay (actual behaviour is stronger than hypothetical interest)
- Follow-up permission and date
- Researcher notes and next action

## Decision thresholds
Proceed to a tightly controlled manual pilot only when:
- 15 customer/family interviews and 15 provider interviews are completed.
- At least 5 providers agree to accept referrals, with service scope and evidence requirements documented.
- There are at least 5 genuine requests from people who understand the service is a pilot.
- At least 3 paid transactions are completed with transparent terms and no unresolved safety issue.

Pause or change direction if people like the idea but will not use/pay, providers cannot meet evidence requirements, requests require clinical/personal care, or safe transportation cannot be confirmed.

## Research ethics
- Participation is voluntary; declining has no effect on access to other services.
- Ask the adult receiving support directly where possible. A family member's interest does not replace the adult's consent.
- Do not promise availability, funding, outcomes, or provider quality.
- Keep identifiable contact details separate from interview notes and delete them when no longer needed.
