# Winnipeg Manual Pilot — Operating Runbook

**Status:** Planning document only. Do not accept bookings until every applicable launch gate below is signed off. Initial pilot area: Winnipeg, Manitoba. The platform is not an emergency, medical, nursing, or personal-care service.

## Pilot model
Start as a human-coordinated referral service. The operator receives a request, checks whether it is in scope, contacts only providers whose service area and capacity fit, shares comparable written offers, and records the customer's choice. The customer contracts directly with the selected provider unless reviewed terms expressly establish another arrangement.

## Request intake: minimum necessary
Collect only:
- Name and one preferred contact method.
- Broad neighbourhood or postal-code prefix (not a full address at the enquiry stage).
- Requested category and a plain-language description of the practical task.
- Preferred date/time window and flexibility.
- Service-specific logistics needed to assess fit (for example, whether a mobility device must be accommodated); do not ask for diagnosis or medical history.
- Whether the requester is the adult receiving the service or is asking on their behalf.
- If on behalf of another adult: record that the adult has agreed to be contacted and the narrow scope of any permission. Do not disclose booking details to a family member until authorization is confirmed directly with the adult or lawful representative.

Do not collect payment card data, health-card numbers, medication details, diagnoses, detailed medical records, or exact access codes in the initial enquiry.

## Triage
1. Acknowledge the enquiry; state clearly that no booking exists until written confirmation.
2. If emergency, immediate danger, missing person, suspected abuse, or urgent medical need: stop normal matching and direct the person to appropriate emergency/local authorities. Do not attempt dispatch.
3. If the request involves clinical care, nursing, medication administration, bathing/toileting, lifting/transfers, ambulance/stretcher transport, or another excluded task: decline and explain the service boundary.
4. Confirm service category, broad area, timing, accessibility logistics, consent, and contact preference.
5. Match only providers with a documented in-scope service, service-area coverage, current required evidence, and confirmed capacity.
6. Obtain a written response from each provider: scope, exclusions, price/estimate, travel charges, availability, cancellation terms, and relevant qualifications/insurance status.
7. Present options neutrally. Do not rank by payment or imply endorsement.
8. Obtain the customer's explicit choice and confirm who is contracting with whom, total price, payment method, cancellation/refund terms, and complaint contact.
9. After the service, ask whether it occurred and invite feedback. Do not claim quality assurance beyond checks actually performed.

## Provider file and review
Before any referral, document:
- Legal/business identity and contact person.
- Exact services offered and prohibited tasks.
- Service area and capacity.
- Applicable licences/permits and how applicability was determined.
- Insurance certificate, coverage limits, expiry date, and whether insurer permits the work.
- Driver/vehicle documentation where transportation is offered.
- Identity/reference/background checks appropriate to role and law, with consent and secure handling.
- Training/first aid/accessibility capabilities where relevant.
- Complaint/incident contact, cancellation policy, and signed provider terms.
- Date checked, reviewer, evidence location, expiry reminders, and review outcome.

Never label a provider "certified", "approved", "safe", or "fully vetted" unless the exact claim is supportable. A document review is not a guarantee of conduct or service quality.

## Transportation-specific gate
Do not refer a ride until Manitoba and municipal requirements have been checked for the exact vehicle, passenger service, driver, accessibility arrangement, and business model. Confirm commercial-use insurance in writing. Confirm mobility-device securement and safe boarding capability. No medical supervision, emergency transport, or stretcher transport.

## Incident and complaint handling
- Make a named operator responsible for receiving reports and maintaining a restricted incident log.
- Immediate danger: direct to 911; do not delay emergency help to complete a platform form.
- Safeguarding concern: prioritize the adult's immediate safety, follow applicable reporting obligations, preserve relevant records, and escalate to the designated lead/legal counsel.
- Pause referrals to a provider while a credible serious safety concern is assessed.
- Acknowledge complaints promptly, document facts separately from conclusions, explain next steps, and do not promise an outcome.
- Notify insurers, regulators, privacy authorities, or law enforcement where legally required.
- Review every incident for corrective action before resuming affected service.

## Cancellation and service confirmation
Publish clear notice periods, provider/customer cancellation rights, no-show handling, weather/disruption rules, refund responsibility, and dispute contact before accepting any payment. Do not take payment until a lawyer/accountant has reviewed the contracting and tax model and the payment flow has been tested.

## Daily operator checklist
- [ ] Review new enquiries and acknowledge them.
- [ ] Screen for out-of-scope or urgent requests.
- [ ] Confirm adult consent and family authorization before sharing details.
- [ ] Check provider evidence and expiry dates.
- [ ] Obtain written scope, price, and availability.
- [ ] Record customer choice and written confirmation.
- [ ] Follow up on completed/cancelled services.
- [ ] Escalate and document complaints/incidents.
- [ ] Restrict access to personal information and clean up records under retention rules.

## Go-live gates — all required
- [ ] Manitoba legal review: marketplace role, consumer terms, privacy, accessibility, contractor classification, screening, transportation, insurance, tax, and liability.
- [ ] Written service boundaries and customer/provider terms.
- [ ] Provider-specific evidence and insurance checks completed.
- [ ] Incident, safeguarding, complaint, cancellation, refund, and breach-response procedures tested.
- [ ] Adult consent and revocation workflow tested.
- [ ] Secure data handling, access controls, retention/deletion, and audit trail implemented.
- [ ] Accessible request and contact route tested with users.
- [ ] Operator trained and backup coverage assigned.
- [ ] Accurate public wording: pilot geography, real service coverage, limitations, fees, and no guarantees.
- [ ] Payment processing reviewed and tested before any live charge.

**No-go rule:** If any required gate is incomplete, keep the site informational and do not accept service bookings or payments.
